What Ferdant does

We are acquiring one independent electrical testing and repair shop in California, and then the next, for long-term ownership. The shops test and repair the transformers, switchgear, circuit breakers, protective relays and motors that utilities, water districts and plants run on.

Why it matters

Electricity use is rising again, and in 2025 the average wait for a new power transformer was 128 weeks, so the one already in service has to be tested and kept running. Since January 2023 the national maintenance standard, NFPA 70B, requires a written program of testing with records. The shops that do the testing are small and often still run by their founders. Ferdant exists so a good shop outlives its founder.

Who we want

People who care whether a transformer stays in service and whether a good shop stays good. Without that interest, the job is lists and telephone calls. With it, the lists have a purpose. We would rather hire nobody than hire someone who does not understand what Ferdant is doing.

We also want people who use AI every day and have built something of their own with it. A project you can link tells us more than a grade.

The terms

  • $20 to $24 an hour. $24 to $30 an hour for the AI Automation Intern.
  • 10 to 15 hours a week during the quarter, lighter through finals, more over winter break (December 12 to January 3).
  • Davis students and recent graduates preferred. Most of the work is remote; some is in person in Davis and at shops in Northern California.
  • A paid trial task of about three hours comes first.
  • You must be authorized to work in the United States.

Business Development Intern

About the role

Ferdant needs conversations with the owners of independent testing and motor repair shops. Owners are hard to reach. They are in the field, and they ignore form letters. A letter written by hand about the owner's own shop, followed by a telephone call and a visit, is how a small buyer gets heard. You write the letters, make the calls and book the conversations. The founder takes every conversation with an owner.

What you will do

  • Write 20 letters a week by hand, each opening with one true fact about the shop: the year it started, a certification it holds, a job it is known for
  • Deliver letters in person to shops within a day's drive of Davis: Sacramento, Stockton, Modesto and the Bay Area
  • Telephone each shop seven days after the letter and ask for the owner by name
  • Book video calls of 20 to 30 minutes and shop visits for the founder, then confirm and rebook
  • Write a one-page card before each call: who the owner is, what the shop does, and the one question we need answered
  • Log every letter, call and reply in the contact sheet the same day

You may be a good fit if you

  • Write a clear three-sentence note on the first try, in neat handwriting
  • Can walk into a shop or telephone a stranger and ask for ten minutes
  • Have a car and a driver's licence
  • Keep exact records without being asked
  • Use AI to research a shop before you write, and check every fact against its source
  • Have held a job dealing with customers: sales, service, canvassing or recruiting

Your first two weeks

  • 25 handwritten letters delivered or posted, each with a telephone follow-up
  • Every open conversation answered
  • Five new calls on the founder's calendar

Acquisitions Analyst Intern

About the role

Nobody has published a complete list of the independent electrical testing and motor repair shops in California. The facts are scattered across public sources: the NETA directory of accredited testing companies, the EASA directory of motor repair shops, the Contractors State License Board, Secretary of State filings, and the bid results and purchase orders that utilities and cities publish. You assemble them into one list. For each shop you find who owns it, when it started, which certifications it holds and who its customers are. The first shop Ferdant buys will come from your list, and every handwritten letter opens with a fact you found.

What you will do

  • Build the list of independent testing, transformer service and motor repair shops in California from the NETA and EASA directories, licence records and company filings
  • Record for each shop the owner, the year it started, crew size, certifications and named customers, each with a link to its source
  • Find one true fact about each shop that a letter can open with
  • Pull bid results and purchase orders for testing and maintenance work from utilities, cities and water districts
  • File California Public Records Act requests where a record is not posted
  • Flag every shop that is listed for sale, and rank the ten best shops each week

You may be a good fit if you

  • Are careful in a spreadsheet and bothered by a number without a source
  • Enjoy digging through filings and directories until the answer turns up
  • Use AI to pull and structure information, then check it against the original
  • Write short, factual summaries
  • Have taken courses in economics, accounting or statistics

Your first two weeks

  • A sourced list of every shop in Northern California
  • Ten one-page shop profiles
  • Three public purchasing records found and summarized

Engineering Intern, Power Equipment

About the role

The product a testing shop sells is a signed report on a transformer, a circuit breaker or a protective relay. The tests follow published standards: ANSI/NETA MTS for maintenance testing and ANSI/NETA ATS for acceptance testing, with NFPA 70B setting how often equipment must be maintained and NFPA 70E setting how the crew works safely. To judge a shop, Ferdant has to understand the tests as well as the shop's customers do. You read the standards and sample reports and explain in plain language what each test proves, what instruments and certification a crew needs, and where a shop's ability ends.

What you will do

  • Explain what each common test proves and when it is required: insulation resistance, power factor, transformer turns ratio, dissolved gas analysis of transformer oil, contact resistance, protective relay calibration and infrared thermography
  • Map what each shop can do from its NETA accreditation, its instruments and its published work, and note what it sends out to other shops
  • Write the technical questions for owner calls and shop visits
  • Join shop visits in December and take notes
  • Read the specifications in public bid documents and list what a qualified bidder must have
  • Keep a glossary the whole team can use

You may be a good fit if you

  • Study electrical or mechanical engineering and have taken circuits, machines or power courses
  • Can explain a technical point to someone who is not an engineer
  • Use AI to get through a standard faster, and check it against the text
  • Are comfortable in a lab or a shop

Your first two weeks

  • A plain-language guide to the ten most common tests
  • Capability sheets for five shops
  • The question list for the December shop visits

AI Automation Intern

About the role

Ferdant runs its desk work with AI agents: finding shops, reading bid documents, pulling facts from filings and drafting. A few people can then cover every shop in California. The tools have to be right, because one invented fact in a letter to an owner ends the conversation. You build the collectors and the checks, and you turn any task we do twice into a tool that runs by itself.

What you will do

  • Build collectors for the NETA and EASA directories, the state licence lookup and public purchasing portals
  • Turn bid documents and purchase orders into structured rows, each with a link to its source
  • Write checks that catch wrong or invented output, and report the error rate
  • Keep the shared sheets, files and scripts in order and documented
  • Teach the other interns the workflows that work

You may be a good fit if you

  • Use Claude Code, Codex or Cursor every day and have built things with them that you can show
  • Write Python or JavaScript
  • Measure whether the output is right before you call it done
  • Can explain what you built to someone who does not code

Your first two weeks

  • Working collectors for two public directories
  • One document pipeline with a measured error rate
  • A short guide the other interns can follow

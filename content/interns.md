## What {{name}} does

We acquire independent electrical testing and repair shops in California, one at a time, for long-term ownership. The shops test and repair the transformers, switchgear, circuit breakers, protective relays and motors that utilities, water districts and plants run on.

## Why it matters

- Electricity use in the United States rose about 7% from 2019 to 2024, after a decade of decline.
- More than half of the distribution transformers in service, about 40 million, are over 33 years old.
- In 2025 the average wait for a new power transformer was 128 weeks.
- Since January 2023 the maintenance standard NFPA 70B requires a written testing program with records.

So the equipment already in service has to be tested and repaired, and the shops that do it are small and often still run by their founders. {{name}} acquires those shops so they keep running after the founder retires.

## Why work here

- You work directly with the founder.
- Your work is used the week it is handed in.
- You see working shops from the inside: test trucks, motors being rewound, switchgear on the bench.
- You are paid for every hour, including the trial.

## Who we want

- You have ideas, and you can explain and defend them.
- You have built something with AI on your own time (an agent, a tool, a dataset, a site) and can show it.
- You are curious how industry works: how equipment is kept running, and how a shop earns its living.
- You do good work and finish it: complete, on the day you said, with its sources.

## Business Development Intern {#business-development}

### About the role

{{name}} needs conversations with the owners of independent testing and motor repair shops. Owners are hard to reach. They are in the field, and they ignore form letters. A letter about the owner's own shop, followed by a telephone call and a visit, is how a small buyer gets heard. You research each shop, write the letters and book the conversations. The founder takes every conversation with an owner.

### What you will do

- Write letters to shop owners, each about that owner's shop, for the founder to sign
- Deliver letters in person to shops within a day's drive of Davis: Sacramento, Stockton, Modesto and the Bay Area
- Telephone a shop to find the right person and a time, when the founder asks for a call
- Book video calls and shop visits for the founder, then confirm and rebook
- Write a one-page card before each call: who the owner is, what the shop does, and the one question we need answered
- Keep the contact record current the same day

### You may be a good fit if you

- Write a clear three-sentence note on the first try, in neat handwriting
- Can walk into a shop or telephone a stranger and ask for ten minutes
- Have a car and a driver's licence
- Use AI to research a shop before you write
- Have held a job dealing with customers: sales, service, canvassing or recruiting

### Your first two weeks

- Your first letters written and ready for the founder to sign
- Every open conversation answered
- New calls on the founder's calendar

## Acquisitions Analyst Intern {#acquisitions-analyst}

### About the role

Nobody has published a complete list of the independent electrical testing and motor repair shops in California. The facts are scattered across public sources: the NETA directory of accredited testing companies, the EASA directory of motor repair shops, the Contractors State License Board, Secretary of State filings, and the bid results and purchase orders that utilities and cities publish. You assemble them into one list. For each shop you find who owns it, when it started, which certifications it holds and who its customers are. The first shop {{name}} acquires will come from your list.

### What you will do

- Build the list of independent testing, transformer service and motor repair shops in California from the NETA and EASA directories, licence records and company filings
- Record for each shop the owner, the year it started, crew size, certifications and named customers, each with a link to its source
- Pull bid results and purchase orders for testing and maintenance work from utilities, cities and water districts
- File California Public Records Act requests where a record is not posted
- Flag every shop that is listed for sale, and rank the ten best shops each week

### You may be a good fit if you

- Are careful in a spreadsheet
- Enjoy digging through filings and directories until the answer turns up
- Use AI to pull and structure information
- Write short, factual summaries
- Have taken courses in economics, accounting or statistics

### Your first two weeks

- A sourced list of every shop in Northern California
- Ten one-page shop profiles
- Three public purchasing records found and summarized

## Engineering Intern, Power Equipment {#engineering}

### About the role

The product a testing shop sells is a signed report on a transformer, a circuit breaker or a protective relay. The tests follow published standards: ANSI/NETA MTS for maintenance testing and ANSI/NETA ATS for acceptance testing, with NFPA 70B setting how often equipment must be maintained and NFPA 70E setting how the crew works safely. To judge a shop, {{name}} has to understand the tests as well as the shop's customers do. You read the standards and sample reports and explain in plain language what each test proves, what instruments and certification a crew needs, and where a shop's ability ends.

### What you will do

- Explain what each common test proves and when it is required: insulation resistance, power factor, transformer turns ratio, dissolved gas analysis of transformer oil, contact resistance, protective relay calibration and infrared thermography
- Map what each shop can do from its NETA accreditation, its instruments and its published work, and note what it sends out to other shops
- Write the technical questions for owner calls and shop visits
- Join shop visits in December and take notes
- Read the specifications in public bid documents and list what a qualified bidder must have
- Keep a glossary the whole team can use

### You may be a good fit if you

- Study electrical or mechanical engineering and have taken circuits, machines or power courses
- Can explain a technical point to someone who is not an engineer
- Use AI to get through a standard faster
- Are comfortable in a lab or a shop

### Your first two weeks

- A plain-language guide to the ten most common tests
- Capability sheets for five shops
- The question list for the December shop visits

## AI Automation Intern {#ai-automation}

### About the role

{{name}} runs its desk work with AI agents: finding shops, reading bid documents, pulling facts from filings and drafting. A few people can then cover every shop in California. The tools have to be right, because we act on what they find. You build the collectors and the checks, and you turn any task we do twice into a tool that runs by itself.

### What you will do

- Build collectors for the NETA and EASA directories, the state licence lookup and public purchasing portals
- Turn bid documents and purchase orders into structured rows, each with a link to its source
- Write checks that catch wrong or invented output, and report the error rate
- Keep the shared sheets, files and scripts in order and documented
- Teach the other interns the workflows that work

### You may be a good fit if you

- Use Claude Code, Codex or Cursor every day and have built things with them that you can show
- Have run many AI agents at once on one discrete task, and can show what they produced
- Write Python or JavaScript
- Can explain what you built to someone who does not code

### Your first two weeks

- Working collectors for two public directories
- One document pipeline with a measured error rate
- A short guide the other interns can follow

## What the shops do

Utilities, water districts and plants run on transformers, switchgear, circuit breakers, protective relays, motors and generators. Independent shops test each piece, repair it and sign the report that says it is safe to energize.

A typical job is a weekend shutdown at a plant: a crew tests every breaker and transformer on the site, and the plant restarts on Monday with a signed report.

## Why it matters

- **Demand is rising.** Electricity use in the United States rose about 7% from 2019 to 2024, after a decade of decline. Spending on factory construction nearly doubled in three years, and more than 125 gigawatts of data centers are planned.
- **The equipment is old.** More than half of the distribution transformers in service, about 40 million of them, are over 33 years old.
- **A replacement is years away.** In 2025 the average wait for a new power transformer in the United States was 128 weeks, about two and a half years. The transformer already in service has to be tested, repaired and kept running.
- **Testing is now required in writing.** NFPA 70B, the national standard for maintaining electrical equipment, was a recommendation for fifty years. Since January 2023 it is written as a requirement: a written maintenance program, tests on a schedule, and records.
- **The shops are small.** An independent testing or motor repair shop is often still run by the person who founded it. When a founder retires, the choices are a sale to a large national group, a sale to the employees, or closing. We want to be another choice: one owner who keeps the shop whole.

## How we own

- **Long-term ownership.** {{name}} is not a fund with a date by which it must sell. We intend to hold each shop, reinvest what it earns and build it slowly.
- **The shop stays the shop.** A testing or repair shop is its technicians and the customers who ask for them by name. The name on the truck, the crew and the way the work is done stay.
- **Technicians first.** The scarce thing in electrical testing is a certified technician whom a customer trusts to sign the report. We judge a shop by the people who do the work and by whether customers call them back.
- **AI for the desk, people for the field.** We run our own office work with AI tools. The office stays small, so money and attention go to crews, training and test instruments.

## Where the profit comes from

A shop is paid for tests, repairs and maintenance contracts, and a good shop is paid by the same customers year after year. {{name}} earns what its shops earn. We reinvest the earnings in technicians, test instruments and the next shop.

## What we look for

- Acceptance and maintenance testing of electrical power equipment
- Transformer testing, service and repair
- Switchgear and circuit breaker service
- Protective relay testing and calibration
- Motor and generator repair

The customers are electric utilities and municipal systems, water and irrigation districts, food processors, manufacturers and other plants that cannot afford an outage.

The shop is owner-run, with a crew customers ask for by name, repeat work year after year and a record of safe work. We are starting in California and the West.

## How we are structured

{{name}} is the parent company. Each shop we buy stays its own company with its own name, manager and crew. The parent owns the shops, sets the standard for safety and reporting, and decides where earnings go.

## Where we are going

Rising electricity demand, new factories and data centers are the large movements of the next twenty years. {{name}} intends to take part in them as a long-term owner: first electrical testing and repair shops, then other industrial service businesses that utilities and plants depend on.

## Where we are today

We are at the start. We have not bought a shop yet. We are meeting owners, technicians and the people who buy testing and repair work, and we would rather take longer than buy the wrong shop.

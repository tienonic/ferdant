Why did my bill go up?

Start with the change on the page, before guessing at the reason behind it.

Prepared from public guidance · Source check: November 2, 2026

In this guide

01   Compare the same period02   Separate the moving parts03   Check the written offer04   Ask about the unresolved lineSources & scope

Put the previous bill, current bill and written offer side by side. Match the dates first, then find the lines that changed. A higher total is a clue, not an explanation.

Step 01

Compare the same period

Check the start and end dates on both bills. Note a longer period, a balance carried forward or a one-time adjustment separately. Mark changes in service or usage. Keep private copies of the documents you compare.

Step 02

Separate the moving parts

Make three columns: recurring service, usage-based charges and one-time items. Record credits separately. For each change, write the label exactly as it appears and the difference in amount. A different label does not by itself explain a charge.

Step 03

Check the written offer

Find the price period, renewal date and exclusions in the agreement you accepted. A subscription promotion can end at renewal. The FTC advises checking renewal prices and contacting the business when the cost differs from your expectation. Keep any renewal notice with the offer.

Step 04

Ask about the unresolved line

Choose the line you cannot explain. Ask the provider for the period, rate or term behind it and the document that supports it. Save the dated response. For a suspected unauthorized charge, promptly use your payment provider’s official dispute process; this comparison does not replace that process.

Make it your own

A starting point for your message.

Adapt this example privately. Use the business’s verified support channel for account-specific details.

My [service] bill changed from [previous total] to [current total] for [billing dates]. The line labeled [exact label] changed by [amount]. My written offer says [relevant term]. Which rate or term explains this line, and can you link the applicable notice?

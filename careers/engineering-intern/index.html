Engineering Intern, Power Equipment

Explain the work: what each test proves and what a crew needs to do it.

About the role

The product of the businesses we want to own is a signed test report on a transformer, a breaker or a relay. To judge a shop we have to understand its work as well as its customers do. You are the translator. You read the standards and the reports and explain what a test proves, what equipment and certification a crew needs to do it, and where a shop's ability ends. Your work decides what we ask on a visit and whether we believe the answer.

What you will do

  • Explain in plain language what each common test proves and when it is required: insulation resistance, power factor, turns ratio, dissolved gas analysis, contact resistance, relay calibration, infrared inspection
  • Map what a shop can do from its certifications, equipment and published work, and note what it sends out to others
  • Write the technical questions for owner calls and visits, come on visits in December and take notes
  • Read the specifications in public bid documents and say what a qualified bidder must have
  • Keep a glossary and a reference shelf the whole team can use

You may be a good fit if you

  • Study electrical or mechanical engineering and have taken circuits, machines or power courses
  • Can explain a technical point to someone who is not an engineer
  • Use AI to get through a standard faster, and check it against the text
  • Are comfortable in a lab or a shop

Strong candidates may also

  • Have interned at a utility, a contractor or a plant
  • Belong to a student engineering society or an electric vehicle team
  • Have an electrician in the family

Useful backgrounds. Electrical Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, Biological Systems Engineering, Physics.

Your first two weeks

  • A plain-language guide to the ten most common tests
  • Capability sheets for five shops
  • The question list for the December visits

Logistics

Part-time, paid by the hour: $20 to $24 an hour, set by your trial. 10 to 15 hours a week during the quarter, lighter through finals, more over winter break (December 12 to January 3) if you want it. Davis preferred. Starts with a paid trial task of about three hours on real work. You must be authorized to work in the United States.

How to apply

Email nicholas@ferdant.com with the subject "Engineering Intern, Power Equipment - Your name". Send a link to one or two things you built or run; a few lines on how you use AI in your own work (your setup, one thing you made with it and how you checked it); a resume or LinkedIn page; and three or four sentences on why you want to do this work, in your own words and written without AI.

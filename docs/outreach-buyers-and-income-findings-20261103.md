# Ferdant outreach buyers and near term income findings

November 3, 2026. Research findings and proposed sales tests; no customer, purchase, grant eligibility or revenue has been established.

## Finding and correction to the offer

The strongest current income hypothesis is a service that helps organizations prepare and maintain useful local information. A generic preparedness packet has a weak sales case because official materials are already available free, and some grants require their use. Test a specific gap in local customization, verification or upkeep before offering a paid packet.

The potential customer has three things: an obligation to deliver outreach, a costly information-maintenance problem and authority to buy help. A grant award by itself establishes none of the latter two. Accessibility can help an organization reach its audience, but does not automatically make a packet worth buying or generate additional funding.

This note develops the [audience and funding research plan](audience-and-funding-research-plan-20261103.md) and qualifies its proposed resource-pack offer. It follows the [comparable website analysis](comparable-websites-20261103.md). The existing six-week research sequence remains proposed; the narrower four-week sales test below is conditional on finding a useful, permitted offer and authorized outreach.

## What an outreach budget pays for

An outreach budget allocates money to reaching people and helping them take useful actions. Depending on the program and approved budget, work can include staff preparation, workshops, printed materials, language access and outside services. Restricted grant money is not interchangeable with unrestricted operating funds.

The proposed relationship is: a funder supports a program; the organization delivers it; Ferdant could supply a permitted service that helps that delivery. The organization records its actual work and results. Strong evidence may support future applications, but buying or distributing packets does not guarantee another award.

A concrete example is the Institute for Public Strategies' September 10, 2026 announcement of a $250,000 Listos regional grant for preparedness work in San Diego, Los Angeles, San Bernardino and Riverside Counties through December 31, 2027. Planned work includes community workshops, household plans and help enrolling in emergency alerts. This establishes a funded activity category, not interest in Ferdant, an available vendor budget or an invitation to approach the organization. [IPS announcement](https://publicstrategies.org/institute-for-public-strategies-awarded-listos/)

## Free alternatives and grant restrictions

Listos publishes free preparedness materials in multiple languages, including accessible PDFs and print formats. Ferdant should compare its proposed deliverable with those materials before assuming a buyer needs another one. [Official resource hub](https://www.listoscalifornia.org/resources/)

The FY2025–26 Listos Southern regional grant supplemental states that Listos-branded materials are required and supplied by Cal OES. Producing an organization's own materials with those grant funds requires prior Cal OES approval and adherence to the specified branding and handbook requirements. Its reporting includes populations and people reached, events and results. These requirements apply to this particular program; do not generalize them to every nonprofit or grant. [Program supplemental, PDF pages 5–8](https://www.caloes.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/Grants/Documents/CFO/FY25-Listos-Regional-SOUTHERN-LL-FINAL.pdf)

The linked 2024 printing policy also describes supplied materials and additional printing within an allocated budget. It is historical context, not confirmation of a current purchasing allowance. [2024 policy](https://mailchi.mp/listoscalifornia/listos-california-2024-printed-materials-policy-and-ordering-procedure)

Implication: an approved local supplement or maintenance service may be worth investigating. There is no established permission to charge this grant for Ferdant materials. The buyer must identify the applicable budget and purchasing requirements before a proposal represents the expense as eligible.

## Who might buy and why

Residents use the information. An organization would pay for the work of making it suitable and keeping it useful.

| Prospective customer | Likely role to investigate | Potential paid need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affordable-housing nonprofit | Resident services manager | Property-specific assistance, utility and preparedness resources |
| Community nonprofit or family resource center | Program director or operations manager | A dependable resource set staff can hand to clients |
| Senior-service organization | Program manager | Readable printed information and current local contacts |
| Property-management company | Property or regional manager | Local welcome and preparedness information for residents |
| Library | Community services manager | Maintained handouts for recurring patron questions |
| City or county | Community services or preparedness staff | Local public information, subject to its purchasing process |

These are buyer hypotheses, not leads or validated demand. Affordable-housing resident-services teams are an initial research priority because repeated questions and several properties could create a maintenance burden. Funded preparedness organizations are another candidate only where official materials leave a demonstrable gap. Government purchasing may take longer and should not be assumed to deliver immediate income.

## Benefits a buyer would need to observe

| Existing work | Potential Ferdant contribution | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Locate correct contacts and registration pages | Source-checked local resource set | Correct jurisdiction, working destination and recorded review date |
| Prepare resources for several locations | Consistent packets from shared records | Fewer preparation hours without missing local exceptions |
| Share information at events or offline | Useful printable and offline copies | Users can complete the intended task in those formats |
| Keep resources current | Defined reviews and corrections | A real update need and affordable maintenance effort |
| Prepare staff to explain the resources | Short facilitator instructions | Less confusion and preparation work in observed use |
| Document outreach | Suitable record templates | Actual counts and results collected by the organization |

This table describes proposed service components, not a claim that Ferdant currently provides all of them. Reporting templates cannot generate evidence of participation by themselves.

An illustrative cost comparison: internal preparation takes 20 hours at an assumed total staff cost of $40/hour, representing $800 of effort. A $500 Ferdant service plus two hours of internal review represents $580. It frees 18 staff hours, but does not necessarily reduce cash payroll. If internal preparation takes only two hours, the purchase is much harder to justify. All rates, hours and savings in this example are assumptions to test.

## A limited offer and price test

Prepare one sample for one location and audience, covering up to 20 reviewed resources, official alert-registration links, printable PDF and offline HTML, source dates, limitations and one revision. Keep essential public information freely available; charge for agreed customization and maintenance. Do not promise live alerts, enrollment completion, professional translation or verified accessibility that has not been delivered and checked.

The conversational price hypotheses were $300–$600 for a fixed-scope packet and $100–$200 per month for maintenance with defined review frequency and support limits. These are unvalidated test ranges, not quotes or market findings. Time the work, include corrections and communication, and calculate whether the price covers delivery before proposing it. Offer recurring maintenance only when the customer has a continuing need.

At an illustrative $500 per packet, three sales would produce $1,500 in one-time gross revenue. If those customers then bought $150/month maintenance, gross recurring revenue would be $450/month. There are no confirmed sales or renewals, and both examples exclude costs.

## Proposed four week action sequence

| Timing | Action | Evidence or output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Compare official free materials with a sample for an area with strong existing source coverage; time preparation | Useful additional service, explicit scope and cost estimate |
| Week 1 | Research 10 organizations with a relevant existing workflow | Reason each may benefit and likely budget-owner role; private contact records stay restricted |
| Week 2 | Through separately authorized outreach, aim for five conversations about existing lists, workload and spending | Recent examples of a problem, current alternatives and a possible purchasing route |
| Weeks 2–3 | If the buyer and usefulness gates pass, prepare two tailored fixed-price proposals | Defined deliverables, eligible payment route and price |
| Weeks 3–4 | Aim for one separately approved paid pilot and deliver the agreed work | Payment receipt, actual effort, task quality and customer feedback |
| After delivery | Investigate ongoing maintenance where justified | A real recurring need and explicit renewal decision |

Counts and dates are proposed trial targets, not commitments or forecasts. A delayed purchasing process can extend the sequence. This is a focused test within the broader research plan, not permission to skip its correctness and usefulness checks.

The buyer conversation should establish what they use now, whether free materials suffice, who updates local information, actual preparation time, remaining funded work, who controls spending and what would prevent purchase. If there is no material gap or permitted payment route, stop pursuing that offer. Having grant money alone is not a reason to buy.

## Honest measures of value

| Measure | What it supports |
| --- | --- |
| Packets printed or distributed | Distribution volume, not readership or distinct people |
| Reliably counted distinct residents engaged | Reach within the stated counting method |
| Correct official registration destination reached | A successful handoff, not enrollment |
| Registration completion separately confirmed | A stronger outcome within the confirmation method's limits |
| Staff time measured before and after | Operational benefit, with comparable work and quality |
| Paid invoice and later renewal | Actual commercial evidence, distinct from interest or a proposal |

Follow the specific funder's engagement definitions; downloads, website events and printed copies cannot be substituted for its required measures. Buying packets does not itself satisfy outreach obligations or secure future grants.

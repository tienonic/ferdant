# Emergent search for Ferdant

November 3, 2026. Proposed feature and experiment.

Ferdant could discover useful combinations of resources for someone's problem, then improve those combinations through reviewed feedback. The immediate search would use straightforward matching rules. The emergent part would be the discovery of reusable patterns through many interactions over time.

This proposal applies ideas from Emergent Garden's [Emergent Complexity](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0HqUYpGQIfs), using the video's English captions. The video explains building blocks and their interaction rules at [3:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0HqUYpGQIfs&t=189), organized complexity producing simpler higher-level structures at [18:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0HqUYpGQIfs&t=1094), experimentation at [27:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0HqUYpGQIfs&t=1661), and unintended consequences at [30:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0HqUYpGQIfs&t=1801). The Ferdant applications below are our proposals, not claims or recommendations made by the creator.

## Start with the person's problem

Example: "I'm in Davis, my power is out, and I need somewhere to charge my phone."

The search needs a few relevant facts: location, immediate need and constraints the person chooses to provide. It should avoid requiring a detailed personal profile.

## Find the individual building blocks

Each resource record needs a source, jurisdiction, purpose, relevant eligibility conditions, contact method, verification date and clear limitations.

| Resource | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| Utility outage page | Official outage information |
| County alert signup | Instructions for receiving local alerts |
| Nearby library | A possible place to investigate for charging |
| Official active facility listing | Current service information, when available |
| Offline contact sheet | Information the person can retain without connectivity |

A library appearing on the map does not establish that it is open, has power or permits charging. The example describes possible resource types, not verified current availability in Davis.

## Apply simple rules to connect them

- Include resources serving the selected location.
- Check eligibility before recommending an assistance program.
- Separate ordinary facilities from confirmed emergency services.
- Mark unknown availability explicitly.
- Prefer current, authoritative information for time-sensitive questions.
- Give a short explanation of why each result fits.

These rules turn a collection of links into a useful sequence: check the outage, investigate a suitable charging option, and save the necessary contacts. A single search that filters and orders resources is ordinary search automation.

## Learn which combinations actually help

People could optionally report that a combination was useful, a resource was unavailable, or an important step was missing. A maintainer would review that evidence. Successful combinations could become reusable plans; failures would suggest changes to the records or selection rules.

A click shows that someone opened a link. It does not prove they received help. Feedback should distinguish finding a destination, contacting a provider and completing the intended task. Avoid collecting sensitive household details.

Ferdant's current recommendation form produces a local draft. Collecting and reviewing submissions would require a new workflow.

## Let useful patterns emerge

Several organizations might independently assemble the same group of resources for residents facing utility interruptions. That pattern could reveal a recurring task that Ferdant had not previously organized around.

The progression would be resource, useful combination, reviewed plan, local adaptation, and improvements to shared resources. Existing branch scopes remain intact; one household problem can draw on several branches.

When a maintainer verifies a correction to a shared record, the correction could update every dependent page and newly generated packet. Printed copies need an edition date and a link to the current version because they cannot update themselves.

Repeated unsuccessful searches might expose a missing record, unclear instructions or an eligibility mismatch. A gap in Ferdant's records does not establish that a community service is absent.

## Keep useful patterns and test small changes

Compare whether a revised plan helps someone find the correct next step faster and with fewer unsuitable referrals. Keep useful changes and revise confusing ones. Popularity alone must not establish factual accuracy, service availability or emergency safety.

A bounded first experiment could use one city or county with strong existing source coverage, about 20 reviewed resources, and three combinations: household preparedness, utility trouble and a resident-services handout. Observe task completion, unsuitable referrals, preparation time and voluntary feedback. These are proposed trial measures, not demonstrated outcomes.

## Keep the site efficient

The first version could use reviewed resource records, simple matching rules and a small library of saved plans. Prepare common relationships and combinations during the site build, load only the relevant county, and filter locally in the browser. Review changes periodically rather than continuously recomputing recommendations on the user's device.

No simulation or continuous AI processing is necessary. Official fault layers, warnings and evacuation guidance retain their authoritative sources; experimentation concerns the organization and usability of resources.

## Connect the proposal to a possible paid service

A housing nonprofit, community center or library might pay for a maintained collection suited to its residents and staff. Reviewed corrections could improve shared records and reduce repeated preparation work across customers. The potential offer is customization, verification, updates and support, while essential public information remains free.

Willingness to pay and lower delivery costs require testing. Buying or distributing packets does not guarantee grant funding. The [buyer and income findings](outreach-buyers-and-income-findings-20261103.md) and [audience and funding research plan](audience-and-funding-research-plan-20261103.md) describe the existing hypotheses and proposed validation work.

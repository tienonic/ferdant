# Ferdant research plan for audience growth and sustainable funding

November 3, 2026. Proposed research sequence; experiments and funding applications have not begun.

Follow-up findings: [outreach buyers and near term income](outreach-buyers-and-income-findings-20261103.md). Free official materials and program-specific grant restrictions narrow the proposed paid offer to a demonstrated gap in local customization or maintenance.

## Direction to test

Test whether Ferdant can earn regular use by making local practical-help tasks easier, then whether organizations will pay to maintain and distribute that useful service. Study donations and memberships as a second route. Large investments and national traffic are later outcomes to explain, not starting targets to copy.

The first candidate task is finding the correct city or county emergency-alert registration page and saving useful local resources for offline use. Compare it with one everyday task, such as finding utility assistance or resolving a billing question. Emergency preparedness can be valuable without frequent visits; an everyday task may produce a different pattern of use. Neither is a selected campus branch or a change to the five existing branch scopes.

The research must answer four questions:

1. Which people have a recurring or important problem that existing resources do not solve well enough?
2. Which route brings those people to Ferdant at a sustainable cost?
3. Who benefits enough to pay, donate or fund the work, and what evidence do they require?
4. Can the team keep the information accurate and useful at that level of demand?

Inputs are the [comparable website analysis](comparable-websites-20261103.md), Ferdant's existing preview and public competitor, customer and funder records. The baseline comparison is commit `5060a695a271daef014c9dcf3450468d1f7def8a`, document Git blob `6ab61ee1e61f9f23cc7252d03cf236007374c19d`. Public sources below were checked November 3, 2026; their publication periods remain distinct.

The bounded output is a decision brief selecting one audience, one acquisition route and one funding hypothesis to test, or explaining why none clears the evidence threshold. Preserve current owners. The existing website owner reviews product decisions; existing outreach owners handle any later approved interviews. This plan assigns no new permissions or personal commitments.

## Phase 1 Explain how the comparables grew

Allow roughly one research week. Build six short case histories: Findhelp, Watch Duty, One Degree, Citizens Advice, iFixit and the California referral/preparedness ecosystem. For each, collect one early-stage record, one later-stage record and one customer-side or independent record where available. Missing evidence stays missing.

Record the initial audience, first useful task, first distribution channel, first payer, funding type, years to the reported scale, staffing and maintenance burden, and any setbacks. Separate confirmed history from explanations proposed by founders. A funding announcement after growth does not show that funding caused the growth.

Use official annual reports, pricing, grant awards, procurement records, archived product announcements and published customer evaluations. Reconcile the mixed traffic periods in the comparison before using traffic for any numerical target. Do not infer acquisition channels from current traffic totals or divide cumulative users by annual revenue to estimate customer value.

Output: six evidence cards and a table of transferable mechanisms. For each claim retain URL, publisher, publication date, measurement period, access date, unit, entity, supporting passage and uncertainty. Hash locally saved public source extracts so later revisions can be identified; do not copy entire archives into the repository.

Stopping condition: each proposed mechanism has a supporting source, a plausible explanation of why it could work for Ferdant and an observation that would disprove it. Drop unsupported mechanisms instead of extending research indefinitely.

## Phase 2 Identify users distributors and payers separately

Allow roughly one week for desk research and preparation. Compare these hypotheses before choosing a pilot geography:

| Role | Candidate | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resident user | Someone preparing a household, moving or handling an unfamiliar service problem | A recent task, current workaround, failed handoff or costly delay |
| Frequent professional user | Library, community-service or assistance staff | Repeated resource-finding work, outdated lists and measurable staff effort |
| Distributor | A local service organization with an existing help desk, website or information packet | A real point in its workflow where a Ferdant link or printable resource would help |
| Paying customer | An organization responsible for maintaining or distributing local information | Budget owner, current spending, procurement route, service requirements and renewal reason |
| Donor or grantmaker | A supporter of preparedness, access or community information | Recent relevant awards, eligibility and the outcomes it actually funds |

Score three candidate areas on documented unmet need, source quality, language/access needs, maintenance cost and a plausible distribution route. Existing checked coverage makes an area easier to test; it does not prove local demand. Keep the umbrella's geography and the campus Discord decision unchanged.

Prepare a later interview sample of 12 residents, six frontline staff and six budget or funding decision-makers across at least three organizations. These are research targets, not scheduled interviews or a representative survey. Avoid recruiting only friends, enthusiastic supporters or people already using Ferdant. Include people satisfied with existing services.

Ask users to describe the last actual task and show their normal route. Ask staff which information they update, how often and how long it takes. Ask buyers who approves spending, what they already pay for, what would block purchase and when renewal would be considered. Ask about real behavior before showing Ferdant. Compliments and hypothetical willingness to pay do not count as customer commitments.

Output: a short audience brief, comparison of current alternatives, proposed interview script and ranked pilot areas. Contact details, consent and raw interview notes belong in the existing restricted workspace, not this repository. Interviews require a separately authorized outreach step.

## Phase 3 Test usefulness before acquisition

Allow two weeks after test participation and scope are approved. Use the existing preview to compare Ferdant with each participant's usual method. Counterbalance which method is tried first and use matched tasks to reduce learning effects. Report individual outcomes and limitations; this small sample cannot establish population-wide demand.

Test three actions: reach the correct official alert-registration destination, find a suitable local resource and save then reopen a useful offline copy. Check municipal exceptions, county fallbacks and an unincorporated area. For assistance, reaching a directory is not receiving a service; for alerts, clicking a registration link is not completing enrollment.

Proposed gates, fixed before observing results:

- All critical destinations in the trial have a recorded jurisdiction and source check. Any wrong-jurisdiction alert route blocks expansion until corrected.
- At least 10 of 12 residents complete the assigned core task without moderator rescue, and no one leaves believing Ferdant enrolled them or provides live emergency alerts. These are usability screening thresholds, not estimates of statewide success.
- Record success, time, confusion and abandonment for both methods. Continue only if there is a clear practical benefit for the chosen audience; faster clicks alone are insufficient when the destination is wrong.
- Measure staff minutes spent checking, correcting and supporting the pilot for two weeks. Set the affordable maintenance limit before the trial and compare actual effort with it.

Output: task results, failure list, corrected prototype requirements and a maintainability estimate. If existing services perform as well with less upkeep, narrow Ferdant to the useful missing step or stop that product hypothesis.

## Phase 4 Test audience acquisition and a payer

Allow two further weeks for initial evidence after the usefulness gate, with longer follow-up where renewal or search visibility requires it. Compare only two acquisition routes initially:

| Route | Small proposed experiment | Success evidence and failure condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trusted distribution | Two approved organizations share a relevant local link or printable resource during normal service work | Count opportunities to share, actual handoffs, observed useful actions and staff effort. Repeated placement matters more than a logo or verbal interest. Stop if it adds work without helping users. |
| Search discovery | Prepare three genuinely distinct, source-checked answers to demonstrated local questions; publish only after the existing release review | Measure impressions, visits and useful actions when approved measurement is available. Observe for 8–12 weeks rather than judging search after two weeks. Rework pages with visits but poor task completion; do not mass-produce city pages with no distinct value. |

Study shareable offline resources as part of those routes, not as a separate campaign. A downloaded file may help several people without further visits; file downloads cannot establish readership. Community sharing remains subject to existing exact-post review and is not permission to post in subreddits or Discord.

For the payer test, prepare two concrete offers: a maintained local resource pack with dated checks and a staff distribution package with onboarding and correction support. Describe deliverables, service limits and renewal terms before discussing a price. Compare them with the buyer's current workload and tools. Do not promise case management, live alerts or integrations that Ferdant does not provide.

Before preparing a proposal, compare free official materials and establish the buyer's authority to pay for the additional work. For the FY2025–26 Listos Southern regional grant, official materials are required and grant-funded custom materials need prior Cal OES approval. Test a local supplement or maintenance gap rather than assuming that grantees need replacement packets. The [follow-up findings](outreach-buyers-and-income-findings-20261103.md#free-alternatives-and-grant-restrictions) preserve the source, scope and implications for the offer.

Advance to a separately approved paid pilot only if at least two independent organizations describe a repeated problem and one budget owner identifies a credible purchasing route for a defined offer. An interview, proposal, letter of interest, signed contract, paid invoice and renewal are separate stages. Record each accurately.

## Funding research and economics

Research these routes in order; the order is a hypothesis about fit, not a funding commitment.

| Priority | Route | Evidence required before pursuing it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Organizational service or subscription | Useful staff workflow, identified buyer, affordable maintenance, purchase route and a reason to renew |
| 2 | Mission-aligned pilot grant or sponsorship | Current eligibility, a defined public benefit, supported delivery cost and reporting requirements; sponsorship must not influence resource ranking |
| 3 | Voluntary donations or membership | Demonstrated value and a reason to support continuing work; preserve free access to essential public guidance |
| Later | Commercial investment | A repeatable paid model, evidence of retention and a credible expansion cost; the comparator's large investment is not an appropriate initial ask |

Screen about 15 funding opportunities from current official program pages and recent award lists; produce up to five qualified candidates, fewer if none fit. Start with community foundations, preparedness programs and institutional community-benefit funding. Record geography, entity eligibility, fiscal-sponsor rules if applicable, current deadline, typical actual award, allowable costs, match requirements, payment timing, reporting, renewal and source. An old award is not an open application. Ferdant's eligibility and legal structure remain unresolved inputs, not assumed nonprofit status.

Build a 12-month cost model from observed work: source checking, corrections, translation, accessibility, support, hosting, distribution, sales effort and administration. Include owner labor and volunteer dependence rather than treating them as free scalable capacity. Separate setup costs, recurring costs and cash received after reimbursement.

Use these calculations with tested inputs:

- Annual contribution per organizational customer = annual price minus delivery and support costs for that customer.
- Break-even customer count = annual shared operating costs divided by positive annual contribution per customer, rounded up. Model one-time onboarding and acquisition costs separately.
- Cost per useful action = channel cash spending plus valued staff time, divided by verified useful actions attributable to that channel. Report the attribution uncertainty.
- Funding gap = planned cash costs minus committed, usable cash receipts. Show restricted grants separately from flexible operating funds.

For scale intuition only, ten customers at an assumed $3,000 annually produce $30,000 gross revenue; 1,000 supporters at an assumed $30 annually also produce $30,000 gross. Neither price, conversion rate nor audience is validated, and neither figure includes costs. A one-time $30,000 grant does not establish recurring income. Test downside cases with half the customers, double the maintenance time and delayed payment before expanding.

## Measurement and decision gates

Use a simple funnel: eligible exposure, visit, location chosen, useful action, and later reuse when another need arises. Track organizational accounts and paying supporters separately from visitors. Do not report referrals as successful assistance, event counts as people or grant prospects as revenue.

Ferdant currently has no analytics system. Early testing can use consented observation and aggregate manual counts. Any later production measurement needs an explicit design and release decision; do not add tracking in this research task. Avoid storing exact location, sensitive queries or private worksheet content. Measure return use only through an approved method, such as a consented follow-up cohort; offline reuse and unobserved sharing remain unknown.

Treat growth as stages rather than promises of millions of users:

| Stage | Evidence needed to advance |
| --- | --- |
| Useful prototype | The task and correctness gates above pass; maintenance effort is recorded |
| Repeatable local distribution | A route produces useful actions in more than one observation period, within the preset effort budget |
| First funded service | A signed and funded pilot or award supports a defined scope; reporting and maintenance are deliverable |
| Sustainable local service | Actual renewal or continuing support, positive contribution and acceptable accuracy over repeated reviews |
| Wider expansion | A second area reproduces the result without unmanageable local customization; larger audience targets follow measured acquisition and capacity |

At each review, decide continue, narrow or stop. Do not use a disaster traffic spike, scraped page count, population served by a county, or large Reddit corpus as proof of retained demand. Funding without a maintenance owner does not pass the expansion gate.

## Deliverables and stopping condition

The full sequence is approximately six research weeks once people and trials are available; search observation and actual renewal require additional elapsed time. This is a sequence, not an installed schedule or deadline commitment.

Keep the working evidence, decisions and unresolved questions in the existing project tracking process. Publish only a reviewed shareable summary. Expected outputs are six comparator evidence cards, one audience brief, a scored pilot-area comparison, task-test findings, two acquisition experiment records, up to five qualified funding opportunities, a cost model and a short decision brief. Each experiment record needs a hypothesis, denominator, baseline, preset threshold, owner, result, limitation and next decision.

The research stage ends when the brief identifies one supportable audience/channel/funding combination and the next bounded trial, or documents why none is justified. This document authorizes no outreach, account creation, collection restart, fundraising submission, payment, tracking deployment or public website release. Repository publication of this plan does not mark any experiment or funding milestone complete.
